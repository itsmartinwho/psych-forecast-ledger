SXSW Dispatch: Psychedelic Medicine Needs to Get Profit Focused.
Less Profitable = Bait for Profiteers
OWEN SCOTT MUIR, M.D.
MAR 10, 2023

Psychedelics are remarkable experiences, I get it. They could transform everything! New perspectives! It’s something the establishment isn’t going to like, with conservative haters like this:

‘I'm not going anywhere near that ship. And I think you should stay away from it, too, Frank.’

Ok, that was actually Timothy Leary before he did too many drugs.

Later, he ruined decades of medical progress that could have been with reckless messaging:

it could give women “several hundred orgasms”, he claimed – and something children could enjoy dealt… life-changing

blows.

Messaging matters. Once you understand new magical stuff thanks to a trip, great. The failure to reckon with the minds of regulators will still doom the enterprise.

The average cost of a drug introduced in 2023, I will remind us all, is $180,000.

Subscribed

There is no accepted rationale to make these drugs cost less. Accessible sounds good, but a drug company that makes the bold choice to not make as much money will be bought by a smart financier who sees the opportunity to charge more. Martin Shkreli may be out for the game, but others aren’t.

If we value these treatments and their outcomes, we need to be more serious about profits, not less. Unprofitable enterprises are prey for profiteers—but only in so much as they are less profitable and can be made more so by slimeballs.

If we care about our patients and the hope we can provide, equitable means an unassailable business model.

—Owen Scott Muir, MD.
